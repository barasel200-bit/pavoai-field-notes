Turning a script into a finished vertical video

Eleven tools, one question: which of them is worth a slot in a real workflow. This page is the map — what each note covers, and the order I would read them in if the goal is to ship a video this week rather than to survey the field.

If you are starting from nothing

Read the first-session walkthrough first. It reduces text-to-video to four decisions and gets a usable clip out of a tool you have never opened. Then read the speed-first notes, because iteration speed determines how many attempts you can afford, and attempts are what quality is made of at this stage.

If you already generate, but the output is inconsistent

The four-slot prompt pattern applies to stills and, with the camera slot swapped for a motion clause, to video. The Korean prompting notes matter if the brief is in Korean, because sentence shape changes what the model does more than vocabulary does.

If the problem is cost or control

Read the open-weight notes. The short version: open models buy reproducibility and fine-tuning, and cost engineering time; hosted APIs buy convenience and cost per clip. Most teams need both, for different projects, and the mistake is picking one ideology for all of them.

If the problem is after generation

The assisted editing notes cover the twenty minutes that follow every clip: silence, captions, reframing, rough assembly. This is where most of the actual hours go, and where the leverage is largest. The reverse search notes cover the other post-production question — where footage came from, and whether a match means anything.

The two genre notes

Game-style clips are a lesson in visual grammar: familiar camera moves make model artefacts forgivable. Family-story songs are the opposite — a workflow where the technology is the easy part and the listening is the work.

What this set is not

Not a leaderboard, and not affiliate content. No tool here paid for a mention, and there are no referral links. Where a tool was tested, the note says what broke; where it was not, the note says so. Model versions move monthly, so treat every observation as a snapshot with a date rather than a permanent verdict — and re-run the comparison yourself before committing a budget to any of it.
