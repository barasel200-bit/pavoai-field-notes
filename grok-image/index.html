Grok AI Image Generator

Stills and reference frames before you commit to video

Image models are cheaper than video models by an order of magnitude, which makes them the right place to settle look and composition. Grok's image generator is fast, follows short prompts well, and produces stills that are good enough to use as reference frames for a video model.

The strength is variety. Ask for six variations of the same scene and you get six genuinely different interpretations rather than six shades of the same image. That is useful when you are still deciding what a shot should feel like, and less useful when you need consistency across a sequence.

For video work, the practical pattern is: generate stills, pick one, then feed it as a reference image to a video tool. It removes a whole class of wasted generations, because you are no longer asking the video model to invent the composition and animate it at the same time.

Where it fits

The tool is listed at grokaiimagegenerator.net. Notes here cover the parts that documentation tends to skip: cost per usable clip, the failure cases, and the point where a different model is the better choice.
