Why game-style clips are everywhere, and what they actually are

Game-style AI clips spread because the visual grammar is already familiar: third-person framing, saturated light, a city that reads as a city at a glance. Viewers recognise the reference instantly, which is why the clips travel further than technically better work with no anchor.

Most of these videos are not running a game engine. They are diffusion models prompted toward a look, sometimes with a reference frame, sometimes with a style LoRA. The tell is motion: engine footage has consistent collision and camera damping, while generated clips drift, slide, or lose object identity when the camera moves too fast.

That does not make them worthless. For mood boards, thumbnail art or a quick joke, the look is enough. For anything claiming to be gameplay, the distinction matters, and the honest label is generated concept rather than capture.
