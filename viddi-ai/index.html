Assisted editing, and the quiet shift away from manual timelines

Assisted editing is the least glamorous part of AI video and the one that saves the most hours. Generation gets the attention; the actual bottleneck in most workflows is the twenty minutes of trimming, captioning, reframing and exporting that happens after the clip exists. Tools that attack that middle layer are worth more than a marginally better model.

What the assisted layer does

Four jobs, in order of payoff. Silence removal, which is mechanical and almost always correct. Auto-captioning, which is now good enough to be a starting point and never good enough to skip review. Reframing for vertical, which is where most time disappears because it needs a judgement call per shot. And rough assembly from a transcript, which turns a one-hour interview into a five-minute first cut that a human then fixes.

Where humans still win

Pacing. A tool can cut silence; it cannot tell that a two-second pause is the point of the scene. Comedy timing, emotional beats, and the decision to let a shot breathe are all outside what current assistants do well. The productive division of labour is: machine does the first 70 percent, human does the 30 percent that carries the meaning, and nobody pretends the percentages are stable across projects.

A workflow that holds up

Transcribe first, edit the transcript, then let the tool cut to the edited text. This inverts the usual order and it is dramatically faster, because deleting a sentence is cheaper than scrubbing a timeline. Keep the original audio untouched until the picture is locked; regenerating captions after every cut is wasted work. Export a low-resolution proxy for review and only render the final at full quality.

Common traps

Auto-captions drift on names and technical terms, and the drift is systematic, so fix it once in a glossary rather than per clip. Auto-reframing crops the wrong subject when two people are in frame. Silence removal eats intentional pauses in scripted content. And every one of these tools is confident when it is wrong, which is why review is not optional.

How to judge one

Give it a real twenty-minute recording, not a demo clip. Measure time-to-first-usable-cut and count the corrections you had to make. If the corrections take longer than editing from scratch, the tool is not ready for that format, whatever the marketing says. The same test applies to search tools and to generation tools.
