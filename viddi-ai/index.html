Assisted editing, and the quiet shift away from manual timelines

Viddi AI points at a real change in editing: the timeline is no longer the only interface. You describe the cut you want, and the tool proposes trims, captions and pacing. It is faster for talking-head footage and social cuts, where the structure is predictable.

The limits appear with material that has no clear script. Documentary footage, interviews with overlapping speakers, or anything where the interesting moment is a pause rather than a sentence tends to confuse assisted tools. They optimise for removal of silence, which is exactly wrong for tension.

The working compromise is to let the tool build a rough assembly and then edit it like any other cut. Treat the suggestions as a first pass from an assistant who has not seen the rushes, not as a finished edit.
