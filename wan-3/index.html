Wan 3

Open video models and the trade-offs that come with them

Wan 3 sits in the open-model lineage, which means you can run it yourself, inspect the weights, and tune inference to your hardware. That freedom is the whole point, and it is also the source of most of the work.

On a single consumer GPU, generation is slow enough that you plan shots instead of experimenting. The quality ceiling is high, particularly for landscape and architectural motion, but getting there requires prompt discipline and a willingness to re-run with different seeds. There is no support line when a clip comes out with a melted horizon.

The practical argument for Wan 3 is cost at volume. Once the pipeline is set up, marginal generations are close to free, and you can batch a hundred variations overnight. For studios with a fixed visual style and an engineer on hand, that trade is worth making; for everyone else, a hosted model is cheaper in hours.

Where it fits

The tool is listed at wan-3.io. Notes here cover the parts that documentation tends to skip: cost per usable clip, the failure cases, and the point where a different model is the better choice.
