Reverse Video Search

Finding where a clip came from, and where the method breaks

Reverse video search takes a clip and looks for near-duplicates elsewhere. In practice it works well when the clip is unedited: same framing, same duration, same compression. It works poorly the moment someone crops, re-times or overlays text, because the fingerprints the search relies on change.

The useful workflow is to search before you edit. If you are trying to credit a source, run the query on the raw file, then on a single extracted frame. Frame search often succeeds where clip search fails, especially for material that has been re-uploaded at a different length.

Treat the results as candidates, not proof. A match means the same visual content exists elsewhere, not that the earlier upload is the original. For anything published, confirm with the uploader or the platform's own metadata before writing a credit line.
