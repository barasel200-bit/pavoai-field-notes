Kling 4.0

Cinematic short clips, with credit burn worth watching

Kling 4.0 is aimed at short cinematic shots: shallow depth of field, motivated lighting, camera moves that feel operated rather than computed. On a good prompt it produces frames that survive a pause, which is a higher bar than it sounds.

The Korean site is straightforward: prompt, reference image if you have one, duration, then generate. References are the useful part. A single still can lock costume, palette and framing across several clips, which is how you keep a sequence looking like one scene instead of five unrelated experiments.

Credits disappear faster than expected because the model rewards retries. A shot that is ninety percent right still needs three more attempts, and each attempt costs the same as the first. Budget for that before committing a sequence to Kling, and keep a cheaper model for the exploratory work.

Where it fits

The tool is listed at kling4.co.kr. Notes here cover the parts that documentation tends to skip: cost per usable clip, the failure cases, and the point where a different model is the better choice.
