AI Video Notes

Working notes from a few weeks of testing text-to-video models, reverse search and lightweight editing tools. Every entry is a tool that was actually used, not a press release.

How these notes were made

Each entry started with a real task: a vertical clip for a product page, a Korean-language promo, a shot that needed to match footage we already had. The tool was used until it either produced something shippable or failed in a way worth writing down. Notes were taken on the same day, which is why they contain specifics — prompt wording, rough timings, the exact moment a face stopped holding — rather than star ratings.

Two rules shaped the set. First, no affiliate links and no paid placements; nothing here was submitted for approval by the vendors, and several of the notes are unflattering. Second, every observation is dated. Video models change monthly, and a note that was true in November may be wrong by spring. Re-run the comparison before committing a budget.

How to read the set

If you are new to generation, start with the first-session walkthrough, then the iteration-speed notes. If output is inconsistent rather than absent, the prompt pattern and the Korean prompting notes are the two that changed results most. If the bottleneck is budget, read the open-weight trade-offs before renting anything.

If the bottleneck is time after generation — trimming, captioning, reframing — the assisted editing note is the one that pays for itself. And if the question is provenance, the reverse search note explains what a match does and does not prove.
