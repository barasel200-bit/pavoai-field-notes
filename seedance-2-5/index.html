AI Video Notes

Seedance 2.5

A Korean-first interface for text-to-video, with smoother motion than 2.0

The first session with any text-to-video tool decides whether there is a second session. Most people lose that session to the interface: too many fields, too many dials, a preview that takes ninety seconds to tell them the prompt was wrong. The fix is not a better model. It is a shorter path from idea to visible frame.

The learning curve is mostly the form

Strip a video tool down to four decisions — subject, action, camera, duration — and the first session becomes fifteen minutes instead of an afternoon. Everything else (negative prompts, motion strength, seed) is a second-session concern. Korean-language users hit an extra step here, because the field labels are usually English even when the tool markets locally; keep a small cheat sheet of the four labels and ignore the rest until you need them.

A first-session walkthrough

Pick a subject you can describe in six words. Pick one action with a visible start and end, such as a door closing rather than a mood. Pick one camera instruction: static, slow push in, or slow pull out. Pick four seconds. Generate three variations and stop. If none of the three reads as motion, the problem is the action verb, not the model — choose something with a clearer physical change and try again.

Prompt patterns that work

Concrete nouns beat adjectives. "Steam rising from a cup on a windowsill, static camera, overcast morning light" outperforms "a cosy and atmospheric scene" every time, in any language. One action per sentence. Camera instructions as a separate clause. Duration stated explicitly, because models fill unspecified time with drift. And a lighting note, since light is the cheapest way to make a generated frame look intentional.

Failure modes worth naming

Two actions in one prompt produce a compromise where neither happens clearly. Abstract emotional language produces a still image with a slow zoom, which reads as a slideshow. Very short durations truncate the action mid-move. Very long durations introduce prop drift. Text in frame is unreliable in every model at this tier, so do not plan a shot around a legible sign.

What "no learning curve" should mean

Not that the tool is simple, but that the first useful output arrives before the user's patience runs out. Judge a tool by time-to-first-usable-clip, not by feature count. On that measure the four-decision approach beats any tutorial, and it transfers to faster models and to Korean-first workflows without relearning anything.
