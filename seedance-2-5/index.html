Seedance 2.5

A Korean-first interface for text-to-video, with smoother motion than 2.0

Seedance 2.5 keeps the Korean-first interface of the previous release, which matters if your team works in Korean and does not want to translate prompt structure in their head. Menus, presets and error messages are all localised, and the prompt box accepts Korean without the odd tokenisation failures you see when a model is English-only.

The jump from 2.0 is mostly in motion coherence. Longer clips hold together: a walking figure keeps its gait, a panning shot does not smear, and objects that leave frame do not reappear in the wrong place. It is not perfect, but the failure rate on a three-to-five second clip is low enough to plan around.

The trade-off is control. There are fewer knobs than on open-model tools, so you cannot tune sampler settings or plug in a custom LoRA. For teams that want a predictable result from a written brief, that is a feature rather than a limitation.

Where it fits

The tool is listed at seedance25.kr. Notes here cover the parts that documentation tends to skip: cost per usable clip, the failure cases, and the point where a different model is the better choice.
